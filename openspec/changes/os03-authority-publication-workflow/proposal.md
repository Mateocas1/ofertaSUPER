# Publish release-scoped catalog authority with governed refresh

## Decision and intent

Authorize an exact release and a versioned catalog-refresh/evidence policy, then keep commercial facts serving through independently verified, policy-conforming refreshes. This closes the missing operator-to-authority bridge without turning every price update into downtime and another human approval.

**Second-round planning contract reconciled; apply NO.** The confirmed user handoffs supersede earlier immutable-dataset consent, per-mutation reapproval, temporal-pointer/time-travel rollback requirements, and contradictory cache-failure wording. The four change specs, `design.md`, and `tasks.md` have since been reconciled to this proposal and independently checked; that planning completion does not authorize implementation or operations. `exploration.md` and `proposalmejora.md` remain historical inputs whose observations are evidence, not current policy authority.

## New policy being proposed

- **Human consent:** one authenticated, scope-authorized user approves initial authority for the exact deployment/release, full commit SHA, scope, expiry, and versioned refresh/evidence policy after independent technical verification. No mandatory second human or service.
- **Continuing authorization:** the initial baseline/candidate digest anchors evidence and the approval contract; it is not permanent consent to immutable catalog bytes. Each conforming refresh requires machine-verifiable evidence and attributable audit, not renewed human consent for every price.
- **Fresh approval boundary:** a new release/deployment, changed scope or validity, policy change, or widening of the permitted refresh class requires fresh independent verification and explicit human approval. Altering an approved baseline/manifest cannot be disguised as a refresh.
- **Continuity boundary:** controlled refresh becomes public only after independent postcommit source verification under the approved policy. Failed pre-public refresh keeps the current verified projection, subject to its existing eligibility and time limits. Outside-policy mutation, unknown governed lineage, missing/invalid required evidence, or failed verification cannot inherit authority.
- **Cache boundary:** immutable positive authority decisions may be cached for at most **30 seconds**, never beyond applicable expiry. Failed/unavailable revalidation MAY continue only under an already-established positive decision until its original absolute deadline; confirmed ineligibility/revocation evicts immediately where observed. At/after that deadline, failure is fail-closed; no sliding renewal. Payload caching still requires verifiable binding and authority revalidation, not blanket cache removal or `no-store`.

These are proposed product requirements, not claims that repository fields or current refresh writers already implement them. Existing manual direct-refresh operational controls remain prerequisites; this proposal adds publication eligibility, not permission to run writers or relax their controls.

## Happy-path quick flow

1. Identify the exact built deployment/release and a real, consistent existing catalog baseline. Assemble reproducible canonical manifest bytes, coverage/watermarks, provenance, and retained evidence; invent no data or history.
2. Independently verify actual baseline facts, evidence linkage, release identity, and the proposed versioned refresh/evidence contract. Present scope, permitted mutations, gaps/degradation, verification result, expiry, revocation, and retention boundaries.
3. Obtain and retain one authorized user's explicit release/policy approval. Atomically publish eligible authority with indispensable verification, approval, audit, and recoverable operation proof through the reusable repository.
4. Read back the persisted identity and current eligibility. **Handing that identity to runtime configuration is a separate authorized configuration step**, not an automatic writer side effect or traffic promotion.
5. Serve protected facts only under that exact authority and verified governed lineage. Independently verify committed source facts and required postwrite/source evidence before public effect; durably establish indispensable evidence/audit before or atomically with that effect. No repeat human consent is needed within the approved contract.
6. Revoke or recover a lost response through attributable operations. A bad promoted data delta requires a separate, independently verified and audited forward corrective generation, not historical-byte selection. Never renew validity or switch authority/release identities implicitly.

**Bootstrap without circularity:** technical verification and initial authority publication must work against the identified deployment artifact and baseline before public traffic or a configured serving authority exists. Trust/scope enrollment and later identity handoff need separate authorization. Environment identity, database access, and the deployment guard supply no approval; the guard consumes authority rather than creating it. Do not embed a release's own future SHA/deployment identity into source as a prerequisite to building that same release.

### Governed mutation and public effect

- **Confirmed design direction:** public commercial reads use a current governed serving projection isolated from mutable source data. Unsupported writers remain source-only and cannot confer public effect. No temporal pointer, time-travel reads, or particular table names are required.
- The approved policy enumerates permitted mutation classes, source/field/scope limits, required verification/evidence, validity, coverage/degradation rules, and accountable actors. Unlisted classes are not implicitly allowed; broader policy needs fresh human approval.
- Each refresh proves its exact affected commercial facts, predecessor governed state, resulting state, policy version, source observations, executor, independent verification result/time, and evidence/audit linkage. Superseded assertions require explicit successor linkage; unknown lineage cannot be relabeled as a valid baseline.
- **Postcommit verification and atomic evidence-before/with-effect:** independently verify committed source facts and required postwrite/source evidence before public effect. Changed protected facts become publicly observable only when their independently verified, policy-conforming lineage and indispensable evidence/audit are durably available. No interval may expose new facts under old evidence, including between verification and publication or during concurrent refreshes. This is an observable outcome, not a trigger, ledger, lock, or storage prescription.
- Failed/unverified pre-public refresh and unsupported source-only writes do not alter the current verified projection; it may continue serving only while independently eligible under its original source-age, policy, authority, and cache limits. Outside-policy mutation of governed serving facts, missing/corrupt required evidence, unknown governed lineage, or confirmed verification failure affecting that projection blocks affected protected facts, including derived and cached representations. If the affected boundary cannot be established, fail closed for the protected commercial surface; static facts may remain. The authority cache's 30-second limit never permits an unverified mutation.
- **Forward-only data correction:** after a bad promoted delta, bounded rollback is a separate, independently verified and audited forward corrective generation derived from retained before-images and checks against the current governed predecessor. It must preserve unrelated intervening verified changes, satisfy the approved policy and current eligibility, and establish evidence before/with public effect. If correction cannot verify, affected protected facts remain unavailable. No temporal pointer rewind, historical-byte selection, or implicit source restoration is required or authorized; an authority/release change still needs its own exact identity and separately authorized configuration handoff.

### Time, caches, and lifecycle

| Contract | Required outcome |
|---|---|
| Truthful degradation | Preserve evidence-backed partial/historical eligibility and existing `last_checked_at`, `PriceHistory.scraped_at`, and historical `verifiedAt` meanings. State coverage unit, expected universe, missing/unknown coverage, and source age truthfully. No invented completeness, minimum percentage, or implicit 24-hour rejection. Partial coverage never excuses missing required evidence or unknown governed lineage. |
| Distinct clocks | Observation, technical verification, approval, activation, refresh, and expiry remain distinct. Verification, consent, retry, and recovery never rejuvenate source data. Design maps shared versus distinct validity limits and activation-time rechecks; refresh does not extend release/policy authorization expiry. |
| Authority decision cache | Any positive decision is immutable and expires at its original absolute deadline, no later than 30 seconds after successful authoritative verification and no later than applicable expiry. Failed/unavailable revalidation MAY continue only under an already-established positive decision before that deadline, with all payload/evidence bindings intact. Confirmed ineligibility/revocation evicts immediately where observed. Without such a decision, or at/after its deadline, failure is fail-closed. Hits, retries, warm processes, DB failure, and unverifiable evidence never renew or extend the deadline; no sliding renewal. |
| Revocation SLO | Across all instances and warm/cache paths, cease newly serving revoked protected facts within **30 seconds of durable revocation**, and always before any earlier expiry. Uncached authoritative checks reject once revocation is visible. Record the durable revocation time and enforcement proof; no additional cache-layer grace. Already delivered content cannot be recalled or represented as freshly verified. |
| Payload caches | Bind payload contents to exact authority, policy, and governed data/evidence identity. Cached API/page/metadata/CDN/service-worker responses cannot bypass required authority revalidation or disguise stale time-dependent prices, promotions, availability, or degradation. A matching envelope alone does not prove payload binding; design selects safe reuse/invalidation, not mandatory cache removal. |
| Atomic activation | Eligible terminal authority, technical metadata, indispensable approval/audit, and operation proof form one atomic outcome with guarded transitions. The final DB-clock check is the authoritative validity decision; atomic commit is the sole public-effect point. Expiry before or at the final check aborts and preserves prior persisted state, not guaranteed serving eligibility. Crossing expiry after the check during durable commit may persist only a historical/ineligible outcome, never serving or active success; prior selection preservation is not guaranteed. Read-back is observational. |
| Failure and recovery | Transaction/indispensable-audit failure preserves prior persisted state, not guaranteed serving eligibility. Unknown commit status requires discovery, not an assumed rollback. A lost response recovers exact committed proof and current eligibility without compensating rewrite, another activation, duplicated activation audit, or renewed validity. |
| Idempotency and competition | Scope retry identity to the authorization operation and compare the entire request. Same key/exact request recovers one result; changed candidate, policy, approval, scope, or other bound payload conflicts. Concurrent operations cannot duplicate activation or ambiguously/silently replace authority; design resolves explicit conflict/supersession rules. |

## Retention, risks, rollback, and non-goals

Retain canonical consent/contract bytes, grant/enrollment proof used by consent, operations/audit, and minimum baseline/refresh evidence and before-images sufficient for independent verification, attribution, and forward correction **through the latest referencing authority expiry + 180 days**. Earlier revocation does not shorten retention; extend only for active references or explicit legal/operational holds. Unpublished/rejected attempts retain required proof through declared validity end + 180 days; unsealed abandoned uploads have bounded deadlines unless held. Design must define custodians and access/deletion rights, integrity and retrieval guarantees, and aligned finite backup/restoration/revocation boundaries. Source-history cleanup does not shorten governed retention. Retained evidence supports verification/correction, not automatic historical serving. Do not require indefinite full-catalog retention; justify any raw/full snapshot storage and exclude unnecessary secrets or personal data. Losing required evidence makes affected authority/data ineligible, not eligible by assertion. Evidence producers, verifiers, approvers, storage custodians, and privileged infrastructure have explicit trust boundaries even when one person holds several roles.

| Risk | Required mitigation |
|---|---|
| Broad policy becomes blanket consent; drift escapes observation | Narrow versioned classes, verified lineage, evidence-before/with-effect, and explicit fresh-consent boundary |
| Cache or overlooked derived output leaks revoked/unverified facts | Complete dependency/surface inventory, payload binding, and measured end-to-end 30-second revocation bound with zero expiry grace |
| Fail-closed controls become routine downtime or misleading freshness | Positive conforming-refresh and partial/historical acceptance; preserve original source times and explicit unknowns |
| Excess storage/custody burden or brittle bootstrap | Minimize retained data, bound retention/access, establish independent trust and release verification without circular configuration |
| Races/recovery corrupt authority; design overbuilds | Atomic indispensable audit, exact recovery/conflicts, bounded verified forward correction with current predecessor checks, and outcome-driven mechanism selection |
| Source/admin privileges bypass governed publication | Mandatory DB bootstrap, effective least-privilege grants/revocations and default-privilege hardening before enablement; unsupported writers remain source-only |

**Rollback:** disable further writer use without weakening the read-only guard; disabling the writer does not revoke authority. Failed pre-public refresh keeps the current verified projection while it remains eligible. After a bad promoted data delta, bounded rollback is a separate, independently verified and audited **forward corrective generation** derived from retained before-images and current governed predecessor checks, preserving unrelated intervening verified changes. If correction cannot verify, affected protected facts remain unavailable; if their boundary is unknown, the protected commercial surface is unavailable. Governed data rollback never selects historical bytes or requires temporal-pointer/time-travel serving. Revoke bad authority through the audited path and meet the revocation SLO. Authority/release rollback still requires independent eligibility, unexpired/unrevoked release-policy and governed evidence, its own exact serving identity, and separately authorized configuration handoff where changed; it never silently selects historical bytes. Preserve required evidence/audit within retention obligations. Migration reversal, source data restoration, identity/configuration changes, and traffic actions require separate operational authorization.

**Non-goals:** new ingestion/schedulers, generic admin UI, mandatory two-person consent, catalog freezes, per-price human reapproval, relaxed direct-refresh controls, manual `PROMOTED` seeding, automatic configuration or traffic promotion. Ed25519, full-catalog retention, universal database triggers/revision counters, ledger architecture, and all-route `no-store` are design alternatives, not proposal requirements.
